The Diagnostic Test

You have diagnosed the problem a dozen times. Wrong roster. Wrong hire. Wrong week. The diagnosis never quite fixes it, because it is aimed at the wrong cause.

This card gives you a different starting point: the seven domains where design problems hide in plain sight, costing you in ways that are hard to name until you know what you are looking for. Nine questions, answered honestly about an ordinary day, and the pattern tells you which part of your operation is carrying the most load.

You get a first move for each of them — something small enough to do this week without a budget or anyone’s permission. What it will not do is tell you which one to move first, or what it is costing you to leave it. That depends on your site, your team and your week, and it is what the free call at the end is for.

Your pattern

Where the load is concentrated

Not a verdict — a measurement. One number for how much your operation absorbs on its own, seven domains showing where it stops absorbing, and the two where a change would pay for itself fastest.

Design Score — questions 01 to 07

A score is a starting position, not a report card. It measures how much of an ordinary week your design absorbs before it reaches a person. It is also the number that moves the moment you change something structural, which is the whole point of measuring it.

The seven domains — where that score comes from

Each scored 0 to 3. The two lowest are where your design is being asked to do the most.

The Base Line — questions 08 and 09, separate from the seven domains

The seven domains score how your operation is built. The Base Line does not score a part of it — it records what an ordinary day feels like right now. It is not added to your Design Score and it does not change your pattern. It is the mark you measure against when you come back.

The two carrying the most

Here is the part worth sitting with: both of those are design problems, and design problems are the fixable kind. They are not about trying harder, hiring better, or caring more. Something in the structure is asking a person to absorb what the system should be absorbing, and structure is the one thing in your venue you can actually change on purpose.
